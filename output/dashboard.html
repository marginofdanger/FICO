Sources & method
  1. Data — loan-level, straight from the GSE disclosure portals. Every figure is derived from the month-end single-family MBS loan-level issuance files: Fannie Mae via PoolTalk® (fanniemae.mbs-securities.com, files FNM_ILLD) and Freddie Mac via its disclosure site (freddiemac.mbs-securities.com, files FRE_ILLD), Dec 2025–Jul 2026. Release timing: both GSEs post each month-end loan-level file on the first business day of the following month (rolling to the next business day when that falls on a weekend or holiday) — July issuance posted Aug 3, 2026, so the next update (August issuance) is expected ~Sep 1, 2026. These are loan-level records (one row per loan) — issuance, penetration, the cross-tab, product mix, and the lender view all come from this single dataset (no pool-level rollups or third-party data), so every view reconciles. Regulatory dates verified against FHFA.
  2. Fields & categories. Per loan we read Classic FICO / VantageScore 4.0 (plus the Origination variants for modified/reperforming loans), Seller Name, loan term, and issuance UPB. A loan "has" a score if it is a valid 300–850 value. VS-scored = VS-only; zero loans carry both — consistent with FHFA, which will not accept a loan delivered with more than one score model.
  3. Denominators & levels. Base = all newly-issued SF loans that month (loan-level count and UPB). Penetration = VS-only ÷ base. Lender-level: each loan attributed to its Seller Name; a lender's rate = its VS loans ÷ its own loans; market share = its loans ÷ all agency loans. Product = by loan term; "pools" = distinct security identifiers.
  4. Loan type. The purpose split reads the disclosure's Loan Purpose field: P purchase, C cash-out refinance, N no-cash-out (rate/term) refinance. A fourth code, M (modified), appears only in reperforming pools — those loans are not new originations, carry no VantageScore, and are excluded from all three purpose lines while remaining in the unfiltered totals (7,245 loans in Jun 2026, none elsewhere). The loan type filter re-cuts the lender and vintage cards entirely within the chosen purpose — the ranking, the penetration denominator and the share-of-market denominator all move together, so a lender's purchase rate and its cash-out rate are directly comparable. Everything above the filter bar (KPIs, headline penetration, cross-tab, issuance, pools, WA FICO, product mix) always covers all loan types.
  5. Issuance month vs origination vintage — why the headline lags. A loan is securitized one to three months after it closes, so any single issuance month blends several origination vintages. During a ramp that materially understates the current run-rate. The Adoption by origination vintage chart re-cuts the same loans by First Payment Date (which falls ~2 months after closing), pooling every issuance file we hold; because each loan appears in exactly one issuance file, the cohorts sum without double-counting. Using Loan Age = issuance month − cohort + 1, a cohort is fed by issuance months cohort−2 (age −1), cohort−1 and cohort; a bar is marked solid only once all three are on hand. Hatched (partial) bars are the fastest-securitized slice of a cohort and should be read as an upper bound — treat the level with care, though the direction of travel is reliable. A ~5% tail at ages 2+ can still trickle into otherwise-complete cohorts.

The pilot. FHFA authorized VantageScore 4.0 for GSE loans on Jul 8, 2025; the disclosure fields went live Nov 17, 2025; and the interim phase — approved lenders may deliver a loan under either Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0 — opened Apr 22, 2026 to a limited set of approved lenders (reported to include Rocket Mortgage, PennyMac, NewRez and roughly 18 other large originators). Being an approved participant is not the same as delivering VantageScore — e.g., PennyMac is on the list but shows ~0 VS deliveries through July (5 loans, 0.09% of its book). FICO Score 10T historical data was published Jul 1, 2026, with adoption to follow.

Important — the score shown is the lender's choice, not the only score pulled. The GSEs still require a three-bureau tri-merge credit report, so a VantageScore-delivered loan almost always also had FICO scores pulled at underwriting. Under lender choice, the originator sees both models and submits whichever it selects as the qualifying score (frequently the one giving better eligibility or pricing). So "VantageScore-only" here means the model the lender chose to deliver to Fannie/Freddie — not that the borrower has no FICO.
